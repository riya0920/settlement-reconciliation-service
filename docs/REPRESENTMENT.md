# SE-2 — which disputes to fight, and whether we still can

Generated by `run_representment.py` against the tiered store built by
`run_retention.py`. The evidence lookups are real: some disputes
resolve out of hot storage, some out of the gzipped archive, and some
cannot be evidenced at all because the retention policy purged the
record.

`src/chargebacks.py` carried a `WINNABLE` set with a comment saying
that knowing which disputes to fight *is the difference between a
chargeback team and a chargeback cost centre*. Nothing used it.

## 1. Can we evidence it at all?

The first question is not *should we fight* but *is the record still
retrievable*. A representment without the transaction behind it is
auto-lost.

| record age | tier that answered | disputes | evidence | fight | fold | cannot evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 days (hot) | hot | 40 | complete | 35 | 5 | 0 |
| 300 days (archive) | archive | 40 | complete | 35 | 5 | 0 |
| 1500 days (cold) | archive | 40 | complete | 35 | 5 | 0 |
| 3000 days (purged) | - | 40 | unavailable | 0 | 0 | 40 |

The bottom row is a storage decision showing up as a chargeback loss.
Those records passed the seven-year legal floor and were purged with
the deletion recorded, which is correct policy — and every dispute
against them is now unwinnable on the merits of transactions that may
well have been perfectly good.

**Read the next section before drawing any conclusion from it.**

`run_retention.py` frames this from the other end: *a retention window
shorter than the observed dispute tail means evidence will be missing
when it is needed*. This is the moment it is needed.

### But that row cannot happen under this policy, and saying so matters

| | days |
|---|---|
| longest scheme dispute window (services not rendered) | 540 |
| legal retention floor, before anything is purge-eligible | 2,555 |
| **margin** | **2,015** |

A dispute arrives at most ~540 days after the transaction. Nothing is
purge-eligible until 2,555. **The gap is 2,015 days, so a legitimate
dispute can never reach a purged record under this policy** — the row
above is a 3000-day-old dispute, which the schemes would not accept.

It is constructed that way on purpose, and the construction is
declared rather than left for a reader to discover. What the row
actually demonstrates is the **mechanism**: what the fight/fold
decision does when evidence is gone. That matters because the gap is a
property of a correctly-set policy and not a law of nature — set
`ARCHIVE_DAYS` from a storage budget instead of from the dispute
distribution and the margin closes. `RetentionPolicy.validate_against`
is the check that catches it, and `run_retention.py` runs it against a
700-day observed tail specifically to show it failing.

Presenting these 40 as a real loss would be inventing a finding. The
real finding is that the policy has 2,015 days of margin and the
machinery to notice if that ever stops being true.

## 2. Is it worth fighting?

| | minor | |
|---|---|---|
| representment fee | 1,500 | charged **whether you win or lose** |
| analyst time | 800 | |
| **cost to fight** | **2,300** | |

The fee is the term people leave out, and leaving it out makes every
small dispute look worth contesting. On a $20 dispute with a $15 fee
and a 40% win rate, fighting has an expected value of **minus $7**.

| | disputes | expected recovery | cost to fight | net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fight | 105 | 1,166,289 | 241,500 | **+924,789** |
| fold | 15 | — | — | 18,219 avoided |
| cannot evidence | 40 | — | — | — |

**Folding is reported as a saving, not a loss.** The alternative was
paying the fee to lose. A chargeback team measured on win rate alone
is rewarded for fighting only the easy ones, and a team measured on
recovery alone is rewarded for fighting everything.

## 3. What order?

**By deadline, not by amount.** A $200 dispute due tomorrow outranks a
$2,000 one due in three weeks, because the second can still be fought
later and the first cannot. Sorting a work queue by value is the
intuitive thing and it loses the winnable disputes at the front.

## 4. What is assumed rather than measured

| reason code | assumed win rate | in WINNABLE |
|---|---|---|
| fraud | 15% | no |
| product_not_received | 55% | yes |
| duplicate_processing | 80% | yes |
| credit_not_processed | 60% | yes |
| unrecognised | 35% | no |

**These are declared stand-ins, not estimates.** This project has no
representment outcomes to fit anything to, and a model fitted to
nothing and quoted to three decimal places would be the worst artifact
in the repo. They are named `ASSUMED_WIN_RATE` rather than
`ESTIMATED_` so nobody quotes them as measurements, and a test asserts
the name.

Everything downstream inherits that. The net figure in section 2 is
arithmetic on an assumption — the *structure* of the decision is what
this demonstrates, and the numbers would move with a real book of
outcomes.

## What this still does not do

- **No evidence document is produced.** The decision says fight and the
  records are retrievable; turning them into the PDF a scheme portal
  wants is a format problem this does not solve.
- **No pre-arbitration.** Losing a representment is not the end of the
  path in the real scheme rules, and the second round has its own
  economics and its own deadlines.
- **Win rate does not vary by evidence quality beyond a halving.**
  Partial evidence halves the assumed rate, which is a placeholder for
  a relationship that is genuinely continuous.
- **The fee is flat.** Real fees vary by network and by whether the
  merchant is in a monitoring programme, and a merchant in one is
  exactly the merchant whose fight/fold arithmetic changes most.
